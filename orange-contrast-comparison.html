Orange contrast, before and after

The brand orange stays the brand orange. The only change is that small text and links use a darker shade of the same colour, so they pass the readability standard. Buttons, price tags, bullets and every filled shape keep the bright orange exactly as it is now.

Why this matters in practice: most of your traffic is on a phone, and a lot of it is people standing outside in Dubai daylight. Orange on white is the first thing that disappears in bright sun. The darker shade reads the same at a glance and stays legible on a screen at half brightness.

The one real decision is the button. Everything above is easy: darker text, no visible downside. The button is a genuine trade-off. White on bright orange fails the standard at 2.94:1, but the bright fill is what makes the CTA jump off the page, and that drives bookings. Three ways to go:

1. Leave buttons bright. Fix only text and links. My recommendation. You get most of the readability gain and the CTA keeps its punch. Buttons are large, bold and unmistakable in context, which is exactly the case the standard is weakest at judging.

2. Darken the button fill too. Everything passes, but the CTA gets noticeably more muted across all 57 pages.

3. Navy text on the orange button. Passes at 3.74:1 for large text, keeps the bright fill, but it reads as a different brand.
